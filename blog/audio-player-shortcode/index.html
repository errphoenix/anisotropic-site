Adding audio to your blog

February 11, 2026
287 words · 2 minutes reading time

Three ways to add audio to your blog: native elements, components, and Howler.js.

There are two practical ways to add audio to a blog: use the native <audio> element or render a custom player with JavaScript. The Zola theme Zolarwind provides two components that map to those choices: audio_simple uses the native element, audio uses a custom player. Both are usable as-is, and the choice comes down to how much control you want over the player UI.

The baseline native <audio>

You get built-in controls and good accessibility. If you are fine with browser-native styling, this is the fastest path.

<audio controls>
  <source src="audio.mp3" type="audio/mpeg">
</audio>

The tradeoff is consistency. Browsers style the element differently, and you get limited control over the UI.

audio_simple component

This wraps the native element and keeps the setup small. It uses no JavaScript, like the baseline audio element, but adds a small amount of CSS for layout.

{{<audio_simple
  config={config}
  src="audio.mp3"
  title="Audio in your blog"
  year="2026"
  artist="Female Speaker"
  label="Blog of Thomas Weitzel"
/>}}

Custom player

If you want a consistent player UI across browsers, the audio component renders a custom player with JavaScript. The native <audio> controls are not used; the UI is fully custom.

{{<audio
  config={config}
  src="audio.mp3"
  length="0:48"
  title="Audio in your blog"
  year="2026"
  artist="Female Speaker"
  label="Blog of Thomas Weitzel"
/>}}

Audio in your blog

Female Speaker | Blog of Thomas Weitzel | 2026

00:00 | 0:48

I prefer the audio component, because I want a stable player UI across browsers. audio_simple stays in the toolbox because it keeps the setup small and uses no extra JavaScript.

Notes

  • The components expect local assets, not remote URLs.
  • zola serve does not support range requests as of 0.22.1, so Chrome cannot seek properly. Use a static server for testing.
